## 1. The story on one page

You came to the town your mother left. She was **Eleanor "Nell" Larkin**, Nana Bea's elder daughter, the laughing girl holding the Copper Kettle's door open with her foot. At twenty-one she went inland with a quiet boy called Frank and became your mother, and for thirty-six years she told him, and you, what you were looking at. She died the autumn before last. Dad has never been to Maple Bay without her narrating, and cannot face it.

**Act One (Chapters 1-5, the first days)** is the family teaching you to live here, one at a time, by doing it with you: Nana feeds you and reads your state off your face; cousin Dez makes you find your own way across town; Pip surveys you about fun; Theo draws your week on a napkin and you get a job; Nora claps in your empty flat and takes you to buy one thing.

**Act Two (6-10)** starts up Nana's attic ladder, where you find a school exercise book in Mom's handwriting: **"MAPLE BAY, FOR F."** The summer she met Dad she began writing her town down for him, a page a place, each with a thing to do there. Thirteen places are listed. Ten pages are written; "The College" stops after two lines; "The Kettle" and "The Lookout" are blank. Nana found the book the week Nell left, read the list, saw that there was a page for a fish market and none for her tearoom, and put it away for thirty-eight years. You walk the pages with the family: the Light with Dez, the fish hall at dawn with Theo, the archive with Pip, Marge the tyrannosaur with Milo. The showpieces are the stages; the play in each Chapter is the thing her page tells you to do.

**Act Three (11-15)** is the blank pages, which are the things nobody said. On the observatory roof Nora tells you the two sentences she has carried for ten years. At the college you learn Mom was one term short of being a teacher, at the school where Nora teaches, and that "a hard town to leave" was praise. In Nana's kitchen the copper teapot, unused since the tearoom closed, turns out to have held Nell's Kettle page for three years: an order slip that says she never left because of the row, and sat out front on every visit only because she thought she had given up the right to the kitchen. At a leaning market stall the bell comes home and Nana and Nora say it out loud. Then the family finishes the book, each writing a page for a place of their own, and Dad comes to be shown. You meet him at Arrivals, where the game began, and this time you are the narrator. At the Lookout you write the last page.

There is no secret and no villain. The mystery of why she left dissolves into the truth: she left because she was asked, and thought there would always be a Tuesday to come back on. What the family mends is the habit that made a mystery of it, **saying the big things with small parcels and never with words**.

Tone: warm, funny, specific; grief present but never milked; the saddest lines are short. Pip and Milo are real children: Pip wants evidence because she is afraid she is forgetting her grandmother's face; Milo doesn't remember her at all and has been lying about it at dinner.

## 2. Canon

### Mom
- **Eleanor "Nell" Larkin** (the school and her mother called her Eleanor; everyone else Nell). Daughter of Beatrice and Raymond Larkin, 4 Thimble Street, Old Town. Sister June is three years younger. The Larkin girls say "Mum"; her own children say "Mom".
- **Who she was here:** loud, funny, bossy, "always halfway out of a door"; carried cups at the Copper Kettle; rode the ferry back and forth to look at the engine boy; marched half the town up the lighthouse; trained as an elementary teacher at Maple Bay University (eight terms of nine; practice term at Maplewood Elementary).
- **The thing she never finished:** the book. And inside it, three things: her teaching certificate ("The College"), her peace with her mother ("The Kettle"), and the last page she was saving ("The Lookout").
- **The book:** bought from Ambrose for thirty-five cents (beaten down from forty). Inside the cover: "Frank will never read this. He would rather be shown. So this is for the days I am busy." She showed him instead, then left with him, so it was never needed until now.

## 3. Each person's arc
- **Nana Bea** (Ch 1, 6, 13, 14, 15): from "I am not F." to reading the note, asking for her bell in writing ("please" underlined), telling Nora the true version instead of the nice one, writing the Kettle page herself, and writing to Frank. Unhealed thing: the row, repeated.
- **Nora** (Ch 3, 5, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15): from "little one" and Thursdays-and-Sundays busy, to "I wasn't there", to the school gate ("Then it can be all of them"), to her own talking. Her page finishes "The College": she is how it ends.
- **Theo** (Ch 3, 4, 8, 11, 14, 15): the out-law with the best seat in the house; ten years of "apology furniture"; he "goes for cocoa" to make room. His wobble becomes a page.
- **Pip** (Ch 3, 9, 14, 15): the investigator who wanted a face on file; ends as the one who makes everybody finish the book.
- **Milo** (Ch 3, 10, 14, 15): from "...hi" to a joke about hats, a memory that is honestly his, and your seat at Sunday dinner. In the finale he tries the stegosaurus question on Dad.
- **Dez** (Ch 2, 5, 7, 15): the fun one who found out about the illness from a fridge calendar; becomes the first to be told things; gets the Railyard its page.
- **Dad** (phone in Ch 7; Ch 15): four words at a time. First asks you to describe the view; in the end asks "What am I looking at" and means everything.
- **You**: a blank the player fills through choices: why you came, what the children call you, which memory of her you give Milo, whether you were angry with Nora, how you count the steps, what you write on the last page. Your arc is the game's: from being shown to showing.
- Kit, the Captain, Ambrose, Roz, Mr. Pike, Mrs. Pym, Silas Marrow each hold one outside piece.

## 7. Festivals and seasons, without putting Chapters on the clock
- No Chapter needs a date. Chapters 7 (dusk), 8 (dawn), 11 (a clear night), 14 (a weekend) and 9/12 (office hours) have natural windows that come round daily or weekly.
- **Variants:** Chapters carry `if` steps on season, weather, time and `festival:<id>`: the stall is dressed for Harvest Festival or the River Walk Fair; snow on the breakwater; the maples "the colour of the jam" at the Lookout in autumn.
- **Festival family moments** (`FAMILY_MOMENTS`, on the clock, optional, a few lines each, standing in the real event): Blossom Parade (Pip on Theo's shoulders, counting floats), Harbor Day (the Captain on the Maple Queen: "Stand at the back rail"), River Walk Fair (the high striker), Harvest Festival (Nana's twelfth ribbon goes to Roz), Lantern Night (Mom wrote shopping lists on wish lanterns), Winter Lights (Mr. Pike brings out No. 9 and rings twice). Each has lines that change with how far the Chapters have gone, so the year and the story meet wherever the player happens to be.
- A player who rushes can finish in about three weeks of game time; one who drifts may reach the Lookout in winter. Both work.

## 8. After the last Chapter
- The game goes on. The book sits at home, readable, with the family's pages and yours; the Map keeps her pins.
- **Standing things:** Sunday dinner on Sycamore Lane (your seat is by Milo); Saturday tea at the Kettle Corner in Roz's bakery (the bell on its old hook); the Captain's Friday dusk (he needs a new hobby); Nana's door.
- **Dad** stays a week (a chair and a window), then turns up at the start of each season: "What's new. What am I looking at." He is a contact you can ring and describe places to.
- **Pinehollow comes down:** Aunt June's scheme in the letters (Granddad at the post office, June at the hotel tea, Robin anywhere that is not Pinehollow, Mrs. Varga up the lighthouse) can be staged later as optional visits; nothing here depends on it.
- **The pages keep coming:** every so often a family member or friend hands you an order slip for a place they love (a ready-made hook for the relationships wave: each vignette can end in a page).
- **Your own pages:** nice-to-have: let the player add a page for any place (pick a line from three), so the book becomes their own guide.

## 9. The data format (short)
`CHAPTERS[i] = { id, n, act, title, hook, ready, marker{place,label}, window{slots,weather,closed}|null, cast[], unlock{after,breath,life?}, minutes, play, teaches[], takeaway[], scenes[] }`; `scene = { id, place, title, steps[] }`.
Steps (`do`): `say` (who, text) · `choice` (id, options[{id, text, reply[], fx{rel, flag}}]) · `go` (place, by, with, label: the player really travels) · `walk` (to, talk[]) · `play` (kind: minigame / activity / buy / ui / climb; id; label; talk[] during; results{good, ok}; skip) · `wait` (event) · `give` (item / recipe / meal / money / ability / contact / recurring / flag / ticket) · `flag` · `rel` · `stage` · `page` (a page of the book) · `if` (cond, then, else) · `beat` (a pause).
Conditions: `time:`, `season:`, `weather:`, `festival:`, `day:`, `flag:`, `pick:<choice>=<option>`, `rel:`, `met:`, `has:`, `done:`, `went:<mode>`; `!` negates. Tokens: `{name}`, `{title}`, `{home}`, `{job}`, `{count}`.
Speakers are cast.js ids (Nora's id is `jules`) plus `SPEAKERS_EXTRA`. The full reference is the header of the data file.
Choices never fail and never branch; every option of a choice gives the same total of relationship points. 68 choices; five are read again later (`c03_title`, `c07_count`, `c07_tell`, `c10_memory`, `c14_bring`); the rest colour relationships and are stored for later writers (texts, vignettes) to use.

## 11. Consistency rulings
- **The lighthouse** is **Gull Point Lighthouse, 97 steps** (98 if you count the doorstep, which Dad does). "Maple Point Light" with **129 steps** is the fictional lighthouse of Lorcan Vey's ghost story in Pages and of the Reel film made from it; Silas says so on screen ("They gave it thirty-two more steps and a ghost. We have neither."). Kit's "four hundred, the way she told it" makes step inflation a town habit. The Keeper who writes letters signs from "Maple Bay Light": read as the Post Office's name for the same tower.
- **Granddad Emmett** is Dad's father: 84, born 1942, joined the Railway Mail Service at 17 in 1959 and served forty years on a line that kept its mail car to the end; married 1961 after two years of daily letters; Frank born 1964. His 1950 parcel-scale story is service lore he was taught, and the "R.P.O. 1954" stamp is from his collection, not his career.
- **The bell:** Nana left it on Ambrose's counter when the Kettle closed (2016); Ambrose kept it four years, between Poetry and Maritime; lent it to the Captain for the Marigold's last crossing (2020). The old beat summary "kept the bell for ten years" is superseded.
- **The paper:** the old newspaper on microfilm is the Bay Courier; the Maple Bay Gazette (Mr. Quill) is today's.
- **The museum** stood on Symphony Street until 2009; that building is Symphony Hall now. Mom's page gives the old address on purpose.
- **Dez** is a Larkin from Ray's side (cast.js says "the other side of the family"; this fixes which).
- **Nora's ids:** cast id `jules`, name Nora. Home ids: cast `home_jules` / place `home:sibling`; `home_nana` / `home:grandma`.
- **"Dinner" or "breakfast":** the intro's invitation leads to Chapter 1, which is a meal at whatever hour you arrive ("It is breakfast when I say it is").
- **The letters stay true:** Dad's "count the steps", "send a postcard of the lighthouse", "practice the narrating" and Aunt June's jam letter all land inside Chapters without requiring the letters. One overlap to watch: Dad's letter 7 ("I might come and see it") and June's letter 7 (her scheme) arrive around days 50-54; if Chapter 15 is already done, those two should switch to a past-tense variant or be held back.
- The surname **Larkin** is Mom's maiden name; the player's surname is never said. (The university's Ashby family is unrelated.)
